Service Management · Chapter 6

Quality is the gap between what customers expect and what they get.

Service quality is judged during delivery, one moment of truth at a time. This module shows how to define it, measure it, design it in, control it with charts, and recover when it fails. Every unit has something to try.

Unit 01

Defining service quality: the five RATER dimensions

LO1

Customers compare the service they expect (shaped by word of mouth, personal needs and past experience) with the service they perceive. They judge on five dimensions.

R · Reliability

Perform the promised service dependably and accurately: on time, the same way, without errors, every time. Includes accurate billing.

A · Assurance

Knowledge and courtesy of staff, and their ability to convey trust: competence, respect, good communication.

T · Tangibles

Appearance of facilities, equipment, staff and materials. Even a noisy guest next door counts.

E · Empathy

Caring, individualized attention: approachability, sensitivity, effort to understand needs.

R · Responsiveness

Willingness to help and give prompt service, including fast, professional recovery.

Measuring quality: SERVQUAL and the walk-through audit

LO1LO7

Service quality has psychological features (ambiance) and can reach far beyond the encounter (health care). SERVQUAL pairs 22 expectation statements with 22 perception statements; perception minus expectation is the GAP 5 score.

Design rules

Flowchart the process first; group statements by stage (ticketing, information, experience, facilities, satisfaction); write declarative statements, not questions; 5-point Likert scale; add a comments box; keep it to two pages.

Administration

Best done in person right after the experience. A reward (gift certificate, discount) raises participation. Mind sample size and cover all customer segments.

As a diagnostic

Give the same audit to customers, staff and managers. Managers see the site daily and miss slow decline (peeling paint, worn rugs) that infrequent customers notice.

Poka-yoke: failsafing both sides of the counter

Shigeo Shingo saw that errors come from interruptions and lapses of attention, not incompetence. Poka-yoke uses checklists or simple devices that make the mistake impossible. Customers co-produce services, so they need failsafes too (Table 6.3).

Server errors

Task: wrong, unneeded, out-of-order or too-slow work. McDonald's French-fry scoop.

Treatment: not acknowledging, listening or reacting. Tellers record the customer's eye color, forcing eye contact.

Tangible: dirty facilities or uniforms, unproofed documents. Mirrors in staff break rooms; spell check before sending.

Customer errors

Preparation: not bringing materials, not understanding one's role. Shouldice's medical questionnaire before admission.

Encounter: not remembering steps, following flow, or specifying wants. Height bars at amusement rides; carry-on size frames.

Resolution: not signaling failure, learning, or doing post-encounter actions. Tray-return stands and bins at the exit.

Unit 05

Quality function deployment: the house of quality

LO4

QFD (from Toyota) translates what customers want into what the firm can change. Rows are customer expectations with importance weights; columns are service elements; cells rate how strongly each element affects each expectation (0–9). Weighted score of an element = Σ importance × relationship.

Textbook check · Village Volvo

Training: (9)(8) + (7)(3) + (6)(5) + (4)(0) + (2)(2) = 127, the highest weighted score. Training also links (strong to weak) with attitudes, capacity and equipment, and is only fourth of five in difficulty to improve. So: invest in training first.

Try it · House of quality for a university IT help desk (illustrative data)

Change any importance or relationship. Watch which element rises to the top.

Roof (correlations between elements):Training ✱ Attitudes (strong)Training • Information system (medium)Staffing ø Facilities (weak)

The nine QFD steps

  1. Set the aim of the project.
  2. Determine customer expectations (rows).
  3. Describe the service elements (columns).
  4. Note correlations between elements (roof).
  5. Rate expectation–element links, 0–9 (body).
  6. Weight the elements using importance (chimney → basement).
  7. Rank improvement difficulty (1 = hardest).
  8. Assess the competition.
  9. Set strategy and goals.

Treat weighted scores with caution and common sense: they rest on uncertain estimates.

Unit 06

The cost of quality

LO2LO8

Juran's four cost categories make the case to top management. In most manufacturers, internal plus external failure costs were 50–80% of the total cost of quality. The rule of thumb: $1 in prevention saves $100 in detection and $10,000 in failure.

Prevention

Keep failures from happening. Quality planning, recruitment and selection, training, improvement projects.

Detection

Check conformance. Inspection, process control, checking and balancing, collecting quality data.

Internal failure

Fix before the customer sees it. Scrapped forms, rework, machine downtime.

External failure

Fix after delivery. Interest penalties, investigation time, legal judgments, bad word of mouth, lost business.

Statistical process control

LO5

Performance varies. The question is whether a change is random noise or a real shift. A control chart plots sample means against ±3σ limits (99.7% of in-control samples fall inside).

Out-of-control signals

A sample mean outside the UCL or LCL, or a run of seven means all above or all below the center line. Then: evaluate, take corrective action, check results.

Variable charts (X̄ and R) for measurements such as time; attribute charts (p) for proportions defective.

Unit 08

Service guarantees and service recovery

LO6LO8

A good unconditional guarantee has five features (Christopher Hart). Test one below; a single missing feature can make customers distrust the whole promise.

Try it · Grade a guarantee

The five features

  1. Unconditional: no exceptions (L.L. Bean takes all returns).
  2. Easy to understand and communicate: measurable (lunch in 15 minutes or free).
  3. Meaningful: matters to the customer (Domino's $3 off instead of a free pizza).
  4. Easy to invoke: no forms or letters (one toll-free call).
  5. Easy to collect: resolved on the spot.

Benefits to the firm: focuses on customers, sets clear standards (FedEx "absolutely, positively by 10:30 AM"), guarantees feedback, forces understanding of the delivery system, builds loyalty.

Complaint-handling policy

Every complaint is a gift. Welcome and encourage complaints, make complaining easy, handle complaints fast and fairly, empower staff, use customer- and employee-friendly systems, reward good handling, keep records and learn.

Service benchmark · Bronson Methodist Hospital

Baldrige-recognized hospital with patient satisfaction at the 97th percentile or better. It listens through surveys, post-discharge calls, focus groups and leader rounds, and invests heavily in staff, which keeps turnover very low.

Key terms, the quality ladder and cases

The service quality ladder (Fig. 6.12)

Firms usually start at the bottom rung, inspection (checking hotel rooms after cleaning), and climb toward quality function deployment at the top, where quality is a basic customer requirement designed into the delivery process. The tools in this chapter sit on the rungs in between.
