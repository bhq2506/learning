Service Management · Chapter 11

An empty seat at takeoff is revenue lost forever.

Once the hotel is built and the aircraft bought, the daily challenge is to match perishable capacity with demand that rises and falls by the hour, the day and the season. This module covers the two generic strategies, tools to shape demand, overbooking, workshift and part-time scheduling, and yield management with the critical fractile.

Our habits create the peaks

Most of us eat at the same hours and take holidays at the same time, so service demand swings between idle servers and waiting customers. Hospitals even see lower utilization in some seasons.

Indonesia's biggest peak

Mudik: in the days before Lebaran, trains, buses, ferries and flights out of the big cities sell out, then run nearly empty in the same direction a week later. Long weekends and school holidays create smaller versions of the same problem for hotels in Bali, Yogyakarta and Malang.

Unit 01

Two generic strategies: level capacity or chase demand

LO1

Level capacity holds capacity fixed and works on demand (marketing tools). Chase demand varies capacity to follow demand (operations tools). Utilities are pure level capacity; call centers that staff agents to the hourly forecast are pure chase. Most services are hybrids: a hotel's rooms are fixed, but staffing varies with the season.

Unit 02

Strategies for managing demand

LO1
Segmenting demand

Demand is seldom homogeneous: business vs. leisure travelers; planned arrivals (commercial clients at the same time daily) vs. random arrivals. Uncontrollable walk-ins can be left as they are while controllable appointments fill the valleys.

Example 11.1 · Redwood Coast Medical Services

Visits peaked on Monday. Subtracting walk-ins from daily physician capacity gives the appointments to book each day: Monday 4, Tuesday 8, Wednesday 10, Thursday 12, Friday 10. Patients wait less; physicians face less stress.

Price incentives, off-peak promotion, complementary services

Offering price incentives

Matinee prices, off-season resort rates, peak-load utility pricing. Ontario's parks proposed four fee levels by expected occupancy: more campers (29,000 vs. 25,977) but less revenue ($59,000 vs. $64,941), plus labor saved on 78 free days. Off-peak pricing fills the valleys with latent demand rather than shifting the peaks.

Promoting off-peak demand

A resort hosts business retreats off-season; a ski resort becomes a backpacking base in summer; "shop early and avoid the Christmas rush"; double coupons on Wednesdays.

Complementary services

A bar for diners waiting for tables; video games in cinema lobbies; heating contractors who also do air-conditioning. Best when the new demand is countercyclical, evening out total demand.

Cheaper weekday cinema tickets, hotel weekday rates, "happy hour" drink prices, and early-bird fares on travel apps all move or create demand in the valleys.

Unit 03

Reservations and overbooking

LO2

Reservations presell capacity, deflect demand to other times or sister facilities, and spare customers a wait. But no-shows leave capacity empty. Non-refundable tickets and 6 p.m. hotel cancellation rules reduce them; overbooking hedges against the rest, at the risk of turning away a customer who holds a reservation.

Example 11.2, Surfside Hotel. A vacant room costs $80 in lost contribution (Cu). Walking a guest to another hotel, plus lost goodwill, costs $200 (Co). Expected no-shows: 3.04 a night, a loss of $243.20 without overbooking.

Build a loss table: on the diagonal (overbooked = no-shows) the cost is zero; above it, $200 for each guest walked; below it, $80 for each empty room. Multiply by the no-show probabilities and add. Overbooking by 2 gives the lowest expected loss, $175.60, a gain of $67.60 a night.

Critical fractile (marginal analysis)

P(d < x) ≤ Cu / (Cu + Co)

Increase overbooking x while the probability that no-shows are fewer than x stays at or below the ratio. Surfside: 80/(80 + 200) = 0.286. P(d < 2) = 0.26 ≤ 0.286, but P(d < 3) = 0.48 is too high, so overbook by 2.

The book prints 0.268 for P(d < 2); its own table gives 0.07 + 0.19 = 0.26. The decision is the same.

Overbooking in Indonesia

Airlines and hotels here overbook too, especially on peak routes and long weekends. The textbook's warning applies: train front-line staff to handle a "walked" guest graciously, arrange an equivalent alternative, and be transparent. Remember the 2017 United Airlines incident, when a passenger was dragged from an overbooked flight: the cost of Co can be far higher than the model assumes once a video goes viral.

Unit 04

Defining capacity and scheduling workshifts

LO3

Service capacity is an achievable output per unit of time for a busy server, or a facility measure (hotel beds, available seat-miles). When demand can't be smoothed (a call center's peak is 125 times its overnight valley), capacity must follow it.

Step 1

Forecast demand

Half-hourly, with weekday/weekend and seasonal patterns (Marin County 911: weekends about 55% of weekdays).

Step 2

Convert to staff

A queuing model sets operators needed so 89% of calls are answered within 10 seconds.

Step 3

Schedule tours

Pick shifts (with breaks) to minimize Σ|Rᵢ − Wᵢ|, required vs. assigned operators.

Step 4

Assign people

Equity in days off and overtime; web-based shift bidding with bonus points and seniority builds perceived fairness.

Weekly schedules with two consecutive days off (integer LP)

xᵢ = number of employees on tour i, whose two days off start on day i (tour 1 = Sunday and Monday off). Minimize x₁ + … + x₇ subject to each day's coverage, e.g.:

Sunday: x₂+x₃+x₄+x₅+x₆ ≥ b₁
Monday: x₃+x₄+x₅+x₆+x₇ ≥ b₂
… Saturday: x₁+x₂+x₃+x₄+x₅ ≥ b₇
xᵢ ≥ 0 and integer

Example 11.4: emergency-room nurses needed Su–Sa = 3, 6, 5, 6, 5, 5, 5 → 8 nurses, with excess on Sunday and Saturday. Multiple optimal solutions usually exist.

Shifts in Indonesian services

Hospital nurses, airport staff, minimarket crews and call-center agents all work rosters like this. Local labor rules on working hours, rest days and overtime, plus Friday prayer time for Muslim staff, become extra constraints in the model, just as union agreements did in Marin County.

Unit 05

More ways to adjust capacity

LO1LO4
Increase customer participation

Fast-food customers order and clear tables: labor arrives exactly when needed. Airlines test boarding orders (rear-to-front, zones, no-carry-on first; Southwest's open seating) like a pit crew.

ID · Self-order kiosks and QR table ordering; self check-in and bag drop at Soekarno-Hatta and Juanda.

Create adjustable capacity

Movable partitions between first class and coach; Benihana's tables of eight with a chef per area; doing side tasks (wrapping cutlery) in slack times to free staff at the peak.

ID · Restaurants that convert a function room into extra dining on weekends; extra counters opened only on payday.

Share capacity

Airlines share gates, ramps, baggage equipment and ground staff at small airports, and lease aircraft to each other off-season.

ID · Ground-handling companies serve several airlines at regional airports; extra aircraft are chartered for the Hajj season.

Cross-train employees

Supermarket stockers open registers when queues grow; cashiers stock shelves when it's quiet. Jobs enlarge in slow periods and specialize at the peak.

ID · Minimarket crews rotate between cashier, shelf restocking and parcel pickup.

Use part-time employees

For persistent, predictable peaks (mealtimes, paydays): students and others wanting extra income, plus paid standby staff at airlines and hospitals.

ID · Students working weekend shifts at cafés, and daily workers hired for Lebaran logistics peaks.

Watch-outs

Part-timers can mean weaker training and loyalty; cross-training costs time; customer participation needs customer training. The textbook asks: what organizational problems can part-time employees create?

Example 11.5 · Scheduling part-time tellers

Meridian Drive-In Bank cut full-time tellers to the level that just meets Tuesday demand and covers the rest with part-timers working 2 to 3 days a week. Three steps:

  1. Minimum part-timers = the larger of the busiest day's part-time need and (total part-time teller-days ÷ max days each). Example: 12 teller-days, max 3 days → 4, but Friday needs 5 → 5.
  2. Decreasing-demand histogram: sort days by part-time need.
  3. Assign tellers in sequence to each block, Friday first, then the next day, carrying the sequence over.

Unit 06

Yield management

LO5

A comprehensive system that combines reservations, overbooking and demand segmentation to allocate fixed, perishable capacity to market segments in the most profitable way. Airlines started it after deregulation; hotels, car rental, cruise lines, rail and freight followed.

When is yield management appropriate?

Relatively fixed capacity

Big investment in facilities; extra demand can only go to a later flight or a sister hotel.

Ability to segment markets

Fences such as a Saturday-night stay separate time-sensitive business travelers from price-sensitive leisure travelers.

Perishable inventory

Each seat or room is a unit of inventory that spoils at departure or midnight.

Product sold in advance

Accept an early discount or wait for a full-fare customer? A demand control chart (±2σ around the expected booking curve) opens or closes discount classes.

Fluctuating demand

Raise utilization in slow periods and revenue in busy ones by opening or closing classes, even hourly.

Low marginal sales cost, high marginal capacity cost

A snack per extra passenger is cheap; a hotel extension comes in lumps of 100 rooms.

The critical fractile for protecting full-fare seats

Example 11.6, Blackjack Airline. Full fare $69, discount $49, 95 coach seats; full-fare demand ~ Normal(60, 15).

  • Cu (too few full-fare seats protected) = $69 − $49 = $20.
  • Co (one protected seat left empty, which could have sold at discount) = $49.
  • P(d < x) ≤ 20/69 = 0.29 → z = −0.55 → protect 60 − 0.55 × 15 ≈ 51 seats.

Holiday Inn · HIRO

A hurdle price per hotel from seasonal patterns, local events and booking pace; filters discount requests when full occupancy is predicted; uses overbooking.

Ryder · RyderFirst

Prices truck capacity by segment elasticity and moves trucks from surplus to demand areas.

Restaurant catering

Software spots low in-store days to push catering and adjusts menu prices with demand.

Amtrak

Tiered fares, overbooking and discount allocation, with flexible capacity by adding or removing cars.

ID · Rail and air

Train and airline fares on travel apps change by booking class as departure approaches; extra cars or extra flights are added for Lebaran, the Amtrak-style capacity lever.

ID · Hotels

Bali and Yogyakarta hotels raise rates for long weekends and New Year and close cheap room types when booking pace runs above the expected curve.

ID · Fairness

Ride-hailing surge pricing during rain or rush hour is dynamic pricing too. Is it fair? The textbook asks students to discuss the ethics of yield management.

Service benchmark · Pay up front and take your chances

Opaque websites (Priceline, Hotwire) sell unsold rooms, seats and cars at bargain prices, non-refundable and "sight unseen" (you learn the hotel or airline only after paying), so firms recover revenue from excess capacity without angering customers who pay normal rates.

Unit 07

Key terms and cases

Case 11.1 · River City National Bank

A fast-growing community bank: a lobby with one commercial and three paying-and-receiving tellers, plus drive-in facilities with full-time morning and part-time afternoon tellers. Peaks around paydays and Friday evenings overload staff while the remote drive-in sits idle; tellers grow surly and customers complain. Recommend a capacity and staffing plan using the chapter's strategies.

Case 11.2 · Gateway International Airport

New commercial service requires air-traffic controller schedules: continuous 8-hour shifts, exactly five days a week with two consecutive days off, within FAA guidelines. Use the hourly and daily demand profiles to build requirements and a weekly schedule, then compare with scheduling hour by hour without those constraints.

Case 11.3 · The Yield Management Analyst

An American Airlines analyst cancels 300+ seats that a sales representative booked for a corporate group's sales-incentive trip to Acapulco on peak flights. Sales wants volume and corporate accounts; yield management wants revenue per flight. Who is right, and how should the conflict be managed?

Case 11.4 · Sequoia Airlines

A regional airline must plan six months of flight-attendant hiring and training (14,000–20,000 attendant-hours a month) with a one-month training period, a probation month, at most five trainees per instructor, juniors limited to 25% of hours, and staff poached by rivals. Formulate it as a linear program.
