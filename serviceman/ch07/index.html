Unit 01

Continuous improvement and the PDCA cycle

LO3

W. Edwards Deming helped post-war Japan export goods of high quality at affordable prices, a combination people thought impossible. His teaching rests on three principles, and his method is a wheel that improves quality a little with every turn.

1 · Customer satisfaction

Put the customer first; satisfying customers' needs is the object of one's work.

2 · Management by facts

Collect objective data and analyze it statistically, so decisions rest on evidence, not opinion.

3 · Respect for people

All employees can be self-motivated and creative; their ideas are sought in mutual respect.

ID · PDCA on campus

Indonesian universities run their internal quality assurance (SPMI) on the PPEPP cycle: Penetapan (set standards), Pelaksanaan (implement), Evaluasi (evaluate), Pengendalian (control) and Peningkatan (improve). Map it to PDCA: which PPEPP step is "Check", and which two together are "Act"?

ID · Quality circles

Many Indonesian companies and state-owned enterprises run small improvement teams (QCC, gugus kendali mutu) that present their PDCA projects at internal and national conventions. These are the "Kaizen Teams" or "Quality Circles" the chapter describes under Lean.

Unit 02

Quality tools for analysis and problem solving

LO1

The chapter follows Midway Airlines, a Chicago hub carrier whose on-time performance was slipping and irritating business travelers. The tools are presented in the order a team would use them.

Check sheet

A historical tally of problem occurrences; today usually a spreadsheet.

Run chart

A variable over time to reveal trends, shifts or cycles, and to compare before vs. after.

Histogram

A frequency distribution. Skew or two peaks (bimodal) hint at underlying causes, e.g., seasonal weather.

Pareto chart

Problems in descending order. The 80/20 rule: a few causes explain most cases.

Flowchart

Ovals for start/end, rectangles for activities, diamonds for decisions. Shows where problems can arise (oversized carry-ons at the gate).

Cause-and-effect

Fishbone or Ishikawa diagram: the problem at the head, categories along the spine (information, customers, material, procedures, personnel, equipment).

Scatter diagram

Two variables plotted together; a tight trend line confirms a suspected root cause.

Control chart

Monitors the process after the fix: is it staying within limits (e.g., on-time above 90%, target 95%)?

Unit 03

Benchmarking and corporate improvement programs

LO2

Benchmarking compares your performance with the "best in class": Singapore Airlines for cabin service, FedEx for overnight delivery, Hampton Inns for clean rooms, Nordstrom for attentive sales staff. It means visiting and learning how they do it, often outside your own industry (manufacturers studying race-car pit stops or Domino's 30-minute delivery).

Five steps:

  1. Select a critical process that needs improvement.
  2. Identify an organization that excels in that process.
  3. Contact the benchmark firm, visit and study the process.
  4. Analyze the findings.
  5. Improve your process accordingly.

Example: an electronics firm improved purchasing by visiting Ford (fewer suppliers), Toyota (vendor relationships) and Reliance Electric (the buying process).

Benchmarking across industries

A hospital pharmacy that wants faster prescription pickup could study a fast-food chain's order-to-counter flow; a university admissions office could study how e-commerce platforms keep buyers informed at every step. Who in Indonesia would you name as "best in class" for queue management? For complaint handling?

Deming's 14-point program

Deming argued management was responsible for 85% of quality problems, so management must lead the change in systems and processes.

ISO 9001

A voluntary standard that has become a de facto requirement, a qualifier, in many industries. Certification signals a quality management system that ensures consistency: "say what you do, and do what you say." Three characteristics: it does not prescribe specific practices; it says nothing directly about the quality of the product or service itself; certification is done by a decentralized system of auditors and accreditation bodies (ISO only writes the standards).

1 · Planning

Goals, authority and responsibility for quality are defined and understood.

2 · Control

Requirements are met, problems anticipated and averted, corrective actions carried out.

3 · Documentation

Objectives and methods documented as objective evidence of system performance.

ID · ISO 9001 as SNI

Indonesia's national standards body (BSN) adopts the standard as SNI ISO 9001. Certificates are common in hospitals, universities, government service units and suppliers to state-owned enterprises, often because a tender or accreditation asks for one. Remember the textbook's warning: certification proves consistency, not that the service is good.

ID · Baldrige-style awards

The chapter preview mentions the Baldrige National Quality Award. Indonesia's state-owned enterprises assess themselves with KPKU, an excellence framework adapted from the Baldrige criteria, which looks at leadership, strategy, customers, workforce, operations and results, not just documented procedures.

Unit 04

Variation and process capability

LO4

Averages hide problems. Six Sigma's aim is to narrow variation until six standard deviations fit inside the customer's limits: the lower and upper specification limits (LSL, USL). For airline arrivals, the customer tolerates ±15 minutes, so Six Sigma needs σ = 15/6 = 2.5 minutes.

Cp · mean centered

Cp = (USL − LSL) / 6σ

Cp ≥ 2.0 is the Six Sigma standard. Arrivals: (15 − (−15)) / (6 × 2.5) = 2.0.

Cpk · mean off-center

Cpk = min[(USL − μ)/3σ, (μ − LSL)/3σ]

If the mean drifts to +1 minute: min[(15−1)/7.5, (1+15)/7.5] = min[1.87, 2.13] = 1.87. No longer Six Sigma, though Cp is still 2.0.

Where specification limits come from

In Indonesia, packaged goods must not contain less than the declared net quantity beyond a legal tolerance (the metrology rules for barang dalam keadaan terbungkus), so a sambal or snack producer has a real LSL. A courier's "next-day" promise, a bank's 10-minute teller-service target and a hospital's 60-minute pharmacy target are service USLs. Ask: who sets the limit, the customer, the regulator or the firm?

Unit 05

Six Sigma and DMAIC

LO5

Motorola engineers began reporting quality as defects per million in the mid-1980s and documented $16 billion in savings. Jack Welch made Six Sigma the cornerstone of GE's legend. The target: 3.4 errors per million customer encounters.

Why count errors

99.9% sounds excellent, but at a million packages a day it is 1,000 errors a day. Reporting errors motivates more than reporting percent success.

The service catch

Perhaps half of a courier's errors are customer-induced (wrong address). A service can't reach Six Sigma unless the quality of customer input improves.

Project-oriented

Top-down support picks projects with the biggest financial benefit: fewer service failures, lower cost, less variability, higher productivity or satisfaction.

  3. Master black belt: expert coach and trainer.

  5. Green belt: leads or supports projects part time.
  6. Project member: contributes process knowledge, and can train up to green belt.

Unit 06

Lean service

LO6

Lean Service extends the Toyota Production System (waste elimination, continuous flow, customer demand pull, known in manufacturing as Just-in-Time) to services. The objective is a continuous, rapid flow of value-adding activities.

Three guiding principles

(1) Do only activities that add value in the customer's eyes. (2) Define the value stream by flowcharting value-added and non-value-added steps. (3) Eliminate waste: anything the customer would not pay for.

Three goals of the perfect process

Right purpose (value): customer-valued activities that are achievable, available, adequate and flexible. Best method (process): low-volume, high-variety flow pulled by demand. Highest sense of accomplishment (people): work that feels valuable and fulfilling.

Value-stream mapping (VSM)

Overall efficiency = total value-added time ÷ throughput time. In Example 7.2, a rural bank's small-business loan takes 95 min value-added + 45 min non-value-added = 140 min, so efficiency is 68%. An online application removes the 30-minute form and two waits (15 + 5 min): 65 ÷ 90 = 72%. Note that the online form still takes the customer's time; it just moves outside the branch.

Techniques for lean services

Rethinking lean after COVID-19

Hospitals asked whether they had become "too lean": with highly uncertain demand, minimal inventories of ventilators and PPE were risky. A better approach is different levels of lean for different supplies (bed linen can stay lean; PPE should not). Layouts needed separate flows, blueprints needed COVID-19 steps, and hygiene habits such as hand-washing may last.

Unit 07 · Supplement

Data envelopment analysis (DEA)

LO7

How do you compare the productivity of branch banks, clinics or schools? Simple ratios (cost per teller transaction) ignore the mix of outputs and inputs, and profit can reflect location rather than efficiency. DEA is a linear program that compares each unit with all the others using multiple inputs and outputs, with no need to convert them to money.

CCR model (Charnes, Cooper and Rhodes). For the unit being evaluated, e, choose output weights u and input weights v that make e look as efficient as possible:

max Ee = Σ ujOje
s.t. Σ viIie = 1
Σ ujOjk − Σ viIik ≤ 0 for every unit k
uj, vi ≥ 0

No unit may exceed 100% with those same weights. Units scoring 1.0 form the efficient frontier; the others are "enveloped" by it. Rule of thumb for sample size: K ≥ 2(N + M) units for N inputs and M outputs.

Reading the results

  • Efficiency rating E: 1.000 = relatively efficient.
  • Efficiency reference set: the efficient peers an inefficient unit is compared with, with weights λ (the shadow prices of their constraints).
  • Composite reference unit: Σλ × peers' inputs and outputs. The gap between it and the unit is the excess input to remove.
  • v values: efficiency gained per unit of input reduced. Burger Palace S4: cut 2 labor-hours (2 × 0.0555 = 0.111) and it matches S3.

Unit 08

Key terms and cases

Case 7.1 · Sonora County Sheriff

A rural California county with one city (about 150,000 people) and a university of 12,000 students. The sheriff's monthly incident check sheet (assault, burglary, disorderly, DUI, theft) suggests burglaries are rising. The data are loaded in the quality-tools workbench.

Questions: (1) Run charts for each category: should she worry about burglaries, and what would you plot against them in a scatter diagram? (2) What is unusual about disorderly and DUI incidents by month, and why? (3) Control charts for assault and theft? (4) Pareto of the year's totals: why might she not prioritize by it?

Think about the calendar

Look at months when students arrive, leave and celebrate. In an Indonesian city, which months would you expect patterns in traffic violations or petty theft (Ramadan, the Lebaran exodus leaving houses empty, New Year)? A Pareto by frequency ignores severity: one assault may matter more than ten thefts.

Case 7.2 · Mega Bytes Restaurant and the Seven-Step Method

A breakfast buffet for business travelers. The Pareto of complaints shows customers wait too long to be seated. Using Joiner Associates' Seven-Step Method: (1) define the project ("waiting to be seated", measured as % waiting over one minute); (2) study the current situation (run chart, flowchart, floor plan); data show tables are unavailable because they are not cleared, and most waiting customers want non-smoking; (3) analyze causes (fishbone: distance from tables to kitchen); (4) implement a solution (temporary work stations in the non-smoking area); (5) check results (a dramatic drop); (6) standardize (permanent stations); (7) plan the future (next Pareto bar: a disorganized buffet).

Lesson: the team had to resist defining the problem as a solution ("too few servers"). The real problem was "customers wait too long."

Case 7.3 · Mid-Atlantic Bus Lines (DEA)

Eight franchised terminals from Philadelphia to Jacksonville; some franchisees complain of poor profits. Outputs: ticket and freight sales; inputs: labor-hours and facility dollars. The data are loaded in the DEA solver: identify efficient and inefficient terminals, recommend input changes using reference sets, advise the one seriously inefficient terminal, and discuss DEA's shortcomings here.

Indonesian adaptation: intercity bus agents (agen PO bus) or courier drop-off points along the Pantura route.

Topics for discussion

  1. Why was Deming's 14-point program rejected by U.S. firms but embraced by the Japanese after World War II?
  2. How can the PDCA cycle support a strategy of low-cost leadership?
  3. What are the limitations of benchmarking?
  4. For a service industry of your choice, what policies are needed for an extended power failure after a severe climate event? (Consider a blackout in Java's grid.)
  5. If Example 7.2's loan process were entirely online, which value-added activities could be eliminated, and what would overall efficiency be?

Interactive exercise: as a class, prepare a value-stream map of a familiar service, such as student registration, and suggest how to eliminate waste.

Service benchmark · Sometimes just hit it with a hammer

A customer's L.L.Bean rolling case had a jammed handle. Customer service replied: give it a good whack with a rubber mallet, and if that fails, return it for an exchange or refund, because every product carries a 100% satisfaction guarantee. The whack worked. Honest, practical advice plus an unconditional guarantee built more loyalty than a replacement would have.
